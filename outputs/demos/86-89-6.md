## Findings (fresh-eyes pass)

1. **With a tall reply, the "You" bubble ends above the viewport (low, by design).** #89 AC 2 asks for the "You" bubble and the reply to be visible without scrolling after a send. That holds while the coach is thinking (0:39). Once the 1,240 px reply lands at its start, though, the "You" bubble's bottom is at y = 4, off-screen. It's the intended trade-off (the reply's start matters more), but AC 2 only holds for replies that fit above the composer.
2. **The Maya swap wasn't exercised by the model (test gap).** v11 names roles ("the account team lead and the billing lead"), not people, so "Person 1" never came back and only "Acme" was restored. A person-name restore on production would need a thread where a person is the subject of a claim.
3. **Guess count not shown on hosted.** All 10 rows were "From thread", so the "N rows are still guesses" clause wasn't seen live. It's covered by tests.
4. **Follow-ups typed with real names show placeholders in "You" (low, UX, #86 D4 as designed).** The visitor typed "Maya asks: does Acme's…" and her bubble reads "Person 1 asks: does Customer A's…", while the reply above it reads "Acme". It's a deliberate proof of what left, but a first-time visitor sees two naming schemes on one screen.
5. **Undeployed follow-up.** `main` has `3d871b8` (the reveal guard starts only when the outcome is outside the space above the composer), which isn't in production's `8522f16`. It didn't matter here: both replies revealed correctly.
6. **Tooling:**
   - Headless Chrome through agent-browser denies `navigator.clipboard.read()`/`readText()` even after CDP `Browser.grantPermissions` (by context or origin) and `Browser.setPermission`. `clipboard paste` and `Meta+V` insert nothing.
   - The real `navigator.clipboard.write` does resolve, so the app's status is genuine. The paste is page-level: a spy keeps the written `ClipboardItem` and inserts each flavour as a paste would. The macOS clipboard stays empty (headless is isolated).
   - The recorder's webm (187.8 s) was shorter than wall time (about 240 s), so the timestamps come from frames, not from a wall clock.
   - In zsh, `for s in $segs` doesn't word-split. Use an array, or the ffmpeg cut silently becomes one segment.
   - `eval` runs in global scope, so a repeated `const` fails. Wrap measurements in an IIFE.
