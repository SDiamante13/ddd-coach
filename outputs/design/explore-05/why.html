  1. What should I ask Dana next?

    Coach
    Three questions for your 30-minute slot, each on one real load.
    • 1. Load 48213 was rebooked with another carrier after a rejection. Same booking, or a new one?
      Why ask it this way?
      The move
      Ask about one real case instead of the general rule. Experts answer a concrete case in seconds and argue about an abstract one for minutes.
      What it unblocks
      Half of your table: if the answer is “same booking”, finance's “cancel plus a new booking” becomes the thing to change.
      Technique
      Example Mapping: test a rule with a concrete example. Source: Cucumber, “Example Mapping” guide.
    • 2. Load 47990 shows AMENDED. What made it AMENDED rather than REBOOKED?
      Why ask it this way?
      The move
      Ask what made the difference, not what the words mean. The difference is where the rule hides.
      What it unblocks
      Your notes and the code disagree here. Her answer tells you which one to believe.
      Technique
      EventStorming hotspot: mark where two sources disagree and resolve it with the person who knows. Source: Brandolini, Introducing EventStorming.
    • 3. Load 48102 got two invoices. Had the first carrier already sent a truck?
      Why ask it this way?
      The move
      You may not need Dana for this. Check the load yourself first and save her time for questions only she can answer.
      What it unblocks
      Whether TONU explains the second invoice, or whether it's the bug finance fears.
      Technique
      Collaborative modeling: bring evidence, not opinions, to the expert. Source: van Kelle, Verschatse, Baas-Schwegler, Collaborative Software Design.
