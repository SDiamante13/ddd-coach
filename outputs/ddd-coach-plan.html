Eazy Freight · one workflow to learn

Confirmed coach briefing: Eazy Freight is a freight forwarder arranging shipments between customers and carriers. For this exercise, focus on booking: a request is submitted to a carrier, the carrier confirms, then the customer is sent a separate confirmation. A submitted booking can be rejected by the carrier and resubmitted. Carrier confirmation and customer confirmation are distinct. Treat these source-derived facts as starting context. Ask the visitor about actors, terminology, gaps, and exceptions; do not invent missing business rules.

Suggested boundary: booking request → carrier submission → carrier confirmation → customer confirmation. Key exception: carrier rejection → revised submission.

Words to clarify: customer, shipper, consignee, carrier, booking request, carrier confirmation, customer confirmation. The coach should ask what these mean to the visitor before treating them as shared language.

Coaching defaults

  • Ask one focused question at a time, using the visitor’s own domain language.
  • Separate confirmed business facts from the coach’s hypotheses.
  • Probe concrete examples and exceptions before recommending a model.
  • For events, ask what happened; for boundaries, ask where language or rules change.
  • Offer short explanations on request; avoid turning every reply into a lecture.
  • Keep uncertainty visible. The visitor supplies business knowledge; the coach brings DDD methods.

Proposed knowledge approach: begin with a small reviewed coaching guide and representative test conversations. Decide on source retrieval after observing specific knowledge gaps.

Scope and decisions

What counts as a useful pilot?

Proposed test: a beginner maps Eazy Freight’s booking workflow, explains the carrier rejection/resubmission branch, and distinguishes carrier confirmation from customer confirmation. Observe whether the coach asks relevant follow-ups, preserves domain language, and recovers from a wrong assumption. Confirm the test duration and success threshold in the interview.

Voice behavior to settle

“Always on” is proposed to mean listening during an explicitly started session. Show microphone state and a persistent pause/end control. Choose whether interruptions should cut off the coach. Decide what happens if microphone permission is denied or speech fails; retain the early text prototype for development, but do not silently restore typing to the final voice flow.
