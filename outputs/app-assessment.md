## Verdict and evidence boundary

DDD Coach is a hosted, password-gated, paste-first coach with a reply contract: ordered events, a table of words that don't match with a holder and a source label on every line, and one question built on two verbatim quotes. The browser renders that contract as structure, restores real names locally, and exports it as RFC Markdown. The server enforces access, grounds general DDD answers in the CC BY Evans DDD Reference behind a citation guard, and pins provider routing so the data notice stays true. Prompt versions ship only through a paired, paid A/B (#78).

What it has not shown is the job it exists for: a real practitioner takes the question to an expert and comes back (#70). Every product-value signal so far comes from synthetic fixtures, public threads or synthetic interviews. The #91 spike also moved the thesis: the question alone isn't what beats a one-line prompt. The structure, safety and memory around it are, and the memory part (#100's living glossary) is built but switched off until its A/B.

Evidence: [production URL](https://ddd-coach.netlify.app), [latest deploy](demos/0926-deploy.md), [#58 demo](demos/58.md), [#86/#89/#6 demo](demos/86-89-6.md), [swaps demo](demos/56.md), [structured reply demo](demos/v11-64.md), [#41 demo](demos/slice-41.md), [key-rotation redeploy](demos/key-rotation-redeploy.md), [eval summary and ship rule](evals/slice-03/summary.md), [v13 A/B](evals/slice-03/ab-2026-09-25-openai-gpt-5-6-terra-v11-v13.md), and spikes [#91](evals/spikes/91-prompt-vs-naive.md), [#92](evals/spikes/2026-09-25-real-threads-92.md), [#93](evals/spikes/2026-09-25-luna-vs-terra-v11.md) and [#98](evals/spikes/2026-09-25-claude-vs-terra-v11.md). ## Current decision record

### What the product has proved

- The reply contract holds on the production model: v11 passed 23/24 hard checks where a one-line prompt passed 0/24 (#91), and v13 shipped through the paired A/B without a gating drop (#78).
- Detection generalises to real public threads (12/12 real splits found, #92).
- `openai/gpt-5.6-terra` is the only tested model that passes the #78 rule on this prompt (#93, #98).
- A visitor can paste, see a structured reply, restore real names locally, and copy it into an RFC without the originals leaving the browser (#56, #86, #6).
- An expired key is now detected within 30 minutes, and a week ahead when OpenRouter reports an expiry (#107).

### What remains unproved or unsafe to claim

- A real practitioner uses the question with an expert and comes back (#70). This is still the top unproved item.
- The coach's question is better than what a practitioner or a plain prompt would ask (#91 says not, on synthetic material).
- The kept glossary improves a second thread: #100 is built, including drift detection and settled rows, but dark behind `GLOSSARY_ENABLED` until prompt v14 passes its A/B, which is blocked on the eval key.
- The coach handles real threads without teams, settled threads and product debates cleanly (#99's gaps are measured, not fixed).
- OpenRouter is acceptable under a target organisation's AI policy (#34: the owner's answer is still pending).
- A shared board beats the structured reply for this job (#94–#97, #20 are open; interview 09 is synthetic).

## Prioritized next decisions

1. **Run the real-practitioner loop (#70) before more surface.** One sanitized real thread, the question taken to an expert, a return visit. Measure whether the question was used and what the visitor had to correct.
2. **Answer #34, the provider path.** The notice and `/data` are now accurate, but if the ICP's policy blocks OpenRouter for work material, the practitioner test itself may be blocked. The options are recorded in #34 and #81; the owner decides.
3. **Restore a working eval key, then run the v14 A/B (#100).** When it passes, turn `GLOSSARY_ENABLED` on. #91 points to memory and structure as the moat, so this is the first test of the memory half. The drift check now requires a verbatim-quoted kept row and fails a drift at a row the thread agrees with.
4. **Then prompt v15 for #99's real-thread gaps.** The $0 checks and synthetic fixtures are in (`fe853e7`, `5bacc97`); "sourced or general practice" becomes gating in that A/B.
5. **Decide the board (#94–#97, #20) on #70's evidence**, not on synthetic interviews. Correction and undo (#95) are the learning-effectiveness gap, so they are the board slice most worth testing first.

The next meaningful proof is unchanged: a stable, attributable question that a real practitioner uses with an expert and can revisit afterward, now with a kept glossary that makes the second visit better than the first.
