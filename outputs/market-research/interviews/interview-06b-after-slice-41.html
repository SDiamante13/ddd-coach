Talks and tools

a phone call on her walk home, 21:40
Q1The last time you tried a tool live during a talk. What happened, and did you ever go back to it?
PR

Context Mapper, a meetup in spring last year. I installed the VS Code extension during the Q&A, typed the speaker's example from the slide, and had a context map in about ten minutes. I went back once, that Saturday, with our booking model, and quit after an hour over the ceremony. So it worked on their example in the room and died on mine at home. What I try in a talk gets tried on the speaker's example. The real test is the return visit, and that only happens if I have my own mess that week.

Q2Walk me through tonight's five minutes.
PR

When the URL and password slide came up, I photographed it, like every slide with a link on it. At the break I typed the URL from the photo and got the password screen. My first try was wrong, because in the photo I couldn't tell if one letter was a capital. It said “Check the slide and try again”, and by then the slide showed the next speaker's title. I checked my photo instead, typed it the other way, and got in: about a minute and a half gone before I saw a message box.

The gate

slice 41 · “Conference password”
Q3Did the password feel like an account?
PR

No. An account wants my email and a promise that I'll remember something. This is the Wi-Fi password: someone puts it on a slide, everyone types it, and nobody owns it. A password on a slide is a room key, not an account. The friction was reading it off a photo, not the gate. And the error told me to check a slide that wasn't there any more.

Nothing to paste

no thread on this laptop · #63
Q4Once you were in, what did you paste?
PR

Nothing, because I had nothing. The pre-read went out this morning, and at lunch I deleted every sanitized copy from the personal laptop. Two weeks of company Slack on a personal machine, names stripped or not, is something I'd have to explain if anyone asked. So I typed three lines from memory, made up to sound like us without being us: “Ops: a rebook is the same booking. Finance: a rebook means a second invoice. Carrier: it's a new tender.” It took about eight seconds and gave me my three lines back as events and words. Its question was about one rebooked load and whether finance raised a second invoice, and that's the pre-read question I sent this morning. It's only as surprising as what you give it, and three lines from memory can't surprise me.

About 150 characters typed. The reply is synthetic, shaped like the slice 3 output: three events, one word (“rebook”) with three holders, and one question. Short input gives the coach no second part of a thread to join.

Q5Would a “Try an example” button have changed those five minutes?
PR

Tonight, yes. I'd have clicked it first. In 04a I said no because I'd come with my own thread, but tonight nobody in that room had theirs, and nobody would paste it on meetup Wi-Fi next to strangers. A three-line example would do what mine did, though: show that it can sort what I said. It has to be long and messy, fifty messages with the “+1”s and a side-thread, with one link hidden between something early and something late, and it has to show me the two lines the question came from, because I won't read fifty messages at a break. The example has to prove the question, or it's an advert for the three things Claude also does.

Q6Suppose the example's reply were prepared in advance, so it's instant and always the good question.
PR

Fine, if the screen says so. If it's canned and doesn't say, the first person who runs their own thread gets my run 2 from Sunday, the advice dressed as a question, and decides the demo was better than the product. A demo that's better than the product is the thing I'd tell people about, and not kindly. Run it live and I'll wait eight seconds.

The notice, in a room

#55 · #69 · on the gate screen
Q7The notice was on the password screen. Was it enough for you to paste anything there?
PR

I read it, because it was the only thing on the screen apart from the field. It's the same notice, still one and a half out of five. But tonight that didn't matter. The Wi-Fi isn't the problem, because the site is HTTPS. The problem is the man next to me with his elbow on my armrest: whatever I paste at a meetup, the row behind me reads too. At a meetup the notice isn't what stops me. The room is.

Coming back

I didn't know it remembered. Nothing said so, and I assumed I'd need the password again, from a photo buried in two hundred slide photos. Q9The plan is to change the password after the conference. That signs out every browser.
PR

Then the 90 days is a promise you break on Monday. Change it if someone abuses it, and cap the money, but don't lock out the people who were in the room. The people who come back a week later are the only ones who matter to you, and they're the ones who'd lose it.

A password field is all the gate needs to say

#41

Keep it. The gate never says the browser stays unlocked, so she assumes she'll need the slide again. And “Check the slide” assumes the slide is still up. Her mistype was a capital, read off a photo.

An example helps first-timers only once replies coach

#63

That gate is met, and the verdict flips for the conference. The new spec: long and messy, one early/late link, the question shows its two lines, not auto-sent, live or labelled as prepared. Add it as an eval fixture, because variance hits the example too.

Conference use is progress on the notice

#55 · #69

No change: still 1.5 of 5, read on the gate. At a meetup the room decides whether she pastes. The notice matters for the return visit from home.

The password bounds the cost, and rotation is harmless

#72

The password spreads by word of mouth in minutes, so the hard limit is the real bound. A spent limit shows “The coach is unavailable. Try again.”, which invites a Retry storm. Rotating after the conference signs out every return visitor. Rotate on abuse, not by date. That's Steven's call.

Conference visitors need their conversation kept

#5

She'd throw the three-line try away. #5 stays a solo-prep need (the Tue → Thu gap from 01). The only thing that must persist from the meetup to home is the unlock.
