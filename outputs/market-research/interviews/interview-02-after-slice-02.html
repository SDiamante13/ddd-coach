It got real. Our VP turned "rough plan by Q1" into "RFC by end of October," so now there's a date with my name on it. Q2Walk me through the last time you prepared for a meeting with Dana. What did you bring in, and what did you leave with?
PR

That was Tuesday, two days ago. Monday night I spent about two hours re-reading the Slack thread and my Confluence page and wrote seven questions in a draft page. Dana gave me 55 minutes on Zoom and we got through three. The first one ate twenty minutes, because she shared her screen and showed me an Excel sheet she made in 2019 mapping every status to what ops actually does. That sheet is the only real documentation of the enum, and nobody knew it existed. I left with answers to three questions, two new questions, a new term (TONU, "truck ordered not used") and a promise that she'd send the sheet. It arrived this morning.

Q3What did you take notes in, and what did those notes look like afterwards?
PR

A plain text file, typing while she talked. It's shorthand, like RB = same bkg unless lane/date chg → AMEND and 2nd inv sometimes legit, TONU. Wednesday morning I could still read it. By Thursday I'd have lost half of it.

Q4Between two Dana sessions, what did you lose or forget?
PR

Mostly the "why." I had "AMENDED??" in my questions list and couldn't remember why I'd flagged it, so I burned two minutes of Dana's time finding out myself. I also lost track of which question came from whom: was the tender one from Ravi or from me reading the EDI code? And I'd used our company Claude to turn my notes from the first Dana call into a clean list, and I couldn't find that chat again among forty others called "Booking notes" or "Untitled." So the loss isn't the notes. It's the thread between the notes and the questions.

Q5What did the last design doc for a service split contain? Where did the diagrams end up?
PR

The last one was the Quotes extraction in 2024. It used our RFC template: context, goals and non-goals, proposed architecture, a data-ownership table, a strangler migration plan, rollout and open questions. There was a box diagram in the draw.io plugin in Confluence, and a sequence diagram someone pasted as a PNG. Nobody updated either after review, and the PNG's source is gone. The glossary section said "TBD" and still does. If I'm honest, the glossary is the section that would have prevented the incidents, and it's the one we skipped.

Q6What's the last thing you actually pasted into a Confluence design doc? What did you have to fix by hand?
PR

Last week, into the Booking RFC draft. First, my seven questions for Dana, copied from a Claude chat. That came through fine, because Claude's copy button gives rich text. Second, a table of the 23 statuses I'd kept as raw Markdown in a text file. Confluence pasted it as pipes and dashes, and I rebuilt it by hand with the table button, which took about twenty minutes. The Quotes data-ownership table came from a Google Sheet, which pastes as a real table. I've never pasted a diagram as anything but a screenshot. What survives in our Confluence is tables and numbered lists. Everything else gets retyped or screenshotted.

Q7What does your AI policy let you paste, and into which tools?
PR

Approved vendors are our Claude Enterprise workspace and GitHub Copilot. Code and internal docs are fine there. Customer names, rates and anything from a customer contract go nowhere, even there. Anything else needs a vendor review with security, and the last one I heard of took six weeks. OpenRouter isn't on the list, and "the text goes to OpenRouter and the model provider" is exactly the sentence that triggers a review. So realistically I'd use your tool with a sanitized thread, like I did in August, or at home with a made-up version.

Q8When you sanitize, what do you actually change?
PR

Customer names become "Customer A," and I strip rates and lane details. I leave colleagues' first names in, which, now that I say it out loud, is probably not great either. It takes ten or fifteen minutes for a long thread. That's annoying, but I do it.

Q9Honest reaction. Did this move toward “I'd use it”?

Q10What about the limits: replies cut off at about 600 tokens, and a 24k-character conversation cap?

My Slack export was about 18,000 characters before I trimmed it. Paste that, go back and forth three times, and you hit the wall. Then the message says "reload to start a new one," which means throw away the work. That's the reload problem again, just triggered by the tool instead of by me.

Q13What exactly would you check, or need to see, before you pasted anything?

This app stores nothing.” If the link holds up, I'd paste sanitized text without the guilt, and I'd have something to forward to security. Add one line telling me what not to paste, like customer names and rates. A tool that tells me what not to paste feels like it's read a policy like mine. And “our server”: whose? Q16The last event is labeled “Coach's idea” with a dashed border. Does that read as clearly tentative?

In a screenshot the dashed border is just another sticky, and in the Markdown it's an italic bracket at the end of a line that nobody reads. If it's a guess, put "guess" at the front of the text.

Rich text on the clipboard, with Markdown as the fallback, because raw Markdown tables arrive as pipes in our Confluence. Three sections, in this order. Roadmap hotspots

Assumptions this interview challenges

Saved sessions wait for evidence of repeat visits

B12 · slice 13 “no persistent history”

Second interview in a row where persistence is the gate. The slice 2 demo shows the gap on video.

OpenRouter is the operator's concern, with no provider choice for visitors

backlog product direction · slice 2a

With an approved-vendor list, a hosted OpenRouter app is blocked for real work, however clear the notice. Aim at sanitized or home use and say so, or find a path through an approved vendor.

One sentence settles data flow

slice 2a notice

The placement is right. The content names a model, not a company. It says nothing about retention at OpenRouter or the provider, or about training, and “our” names no operator. Without a link she can't forward it to security.

Export can wait, or plain Markdown is enough

slice 13 · interview-01 suggestion 4

Her Confluence turns raw Markdown tables into pipes. The minimum export is a rich-text table plus numbered lists, with the term map first.

Public GitHub links are leading context candidates

B17 · B18 · B19

Her monolith is private. Her real second source is a pasted enum and an Excel sheet. Paste beats GitHub for this ICP.

The board starts with events

slice 4 · 4a–4d

The artifact she'd actually screenshot is the term map. An events-only board delays the part she values most.

The 600-token cap is safe because replies stay short

slice 1b · B35

A cut-off untangle (terms, events, questions) costs trust. The reply needs a shape that fits, or a visible “cut short” marker.

The 24k cap is an edge case

B38 · B40 · B41

One real 18k-character paste plus a few turns reaches it. Then the “reload” copy discards the work.

Disagreement is attributed per source

B05

Attributing by person is socially risky. Attribute by team, and treat the expert's answers as unconfirmed notes.

Voice replaces the composer

slice 12

Voice may have a job in the debrief, alongside text and paste, not instead of them.
