The 8,000-character limit

Shown the slice 2a demo at 1:01: a 9,000-character message gets “This message is too long for the coach. Shorten it and send it again.” There's no Retry, and the input is cleared.

Q10Your Slack export was about 18,000 characters. What's it at after sanitizing?
PR

About twelve thousand. Sanitizing removes names and rates, not messages. So even the cleaned-up version fails. And your box is one line. When I paste a Slack thread into a one-line input, the line breaks go and it's one long sentence. You lose who said what, and who said what is the entire point of that thread.

Q11Say you've pasted the twelve thousand and you get this message. What do you do at that moment?
PR

First I'd look for my text, and it's gone from the box. It's in the log above, all twelve thousand characters of it, so I'd be scrolling and selecting by hand on a laptop trackpad. Then I'd have to guess how much to cut, because the message doesn't say what the limit is. I'd delete the first forty messages, the “following” and “+1” and the original question, and keep the argument. That's maybe seven thousand. Another ten minutes, on top of the fifteen for sanitizing. And the part I'd cut first is where ops explains their definition in their own words, which is the part Dana would care about.

Q12When was the last time a tool rejected your input for length? What did you do?
PR

August, our company Claude. I'd attached the Rails model and the enum and a chunk of the callbacks, and after a few turns it said the conversation was too long and to start a new chat. I started a new chat, pasted the half I thought mattered, and lost the connection between the callbacks and the statuses. That's one of the forty chats called “Booking notes.” What I did was retry once, cut, and then stop trusting the answer, because I knew it hadn't seen everything. I'd do the same here. Cut once. If it rejects me twice, I close the tab.

Q13What would you want the message to say?
PR

The number. “Your message is 12,040 characters; the limit is 8,000.” Then I know it's a 35% cut and not a 5% one. And leave my text in the box. Honestly, though, I'd rather it just took the thread. The thread is the input. Everything I type after that is a sentence or two.

Q14If you did get the thread in, a few turns later you'd hit the 24k conversation limit. One idea is to trim or summarize older turns automatically. How would that land?
PR

Depends what “older” means. In my case the oldest turn is the thread, and everything after it is me asking about it. If you summarize my thread to make room for my chat, you've kept the wrong half. Keep the paste, squash the chat. And tell me you did it, in one line, beside the reply.

Your own key, and self-hosting

decision #34
Q15One option on the table: a field where you paste your own Anthropic API key, so your text goes to Anthropic under your company's terms. Is that acceptable?
PR

For me, it doesn't matter, because I can't get one. Our Claude Enterprise is the chat workspace. There's no API key in it for me to copy. API access is a separate account the platform team runs.

Q16How do you know that? Has anyone on your team tried?
PR

Ravi, in July. He wanted to run a script that summarized Jira tickets. He filed a ticket with platform, had to name the service and an owner, and the key went into Vault. He's never seen the key itself. The script reads it at runtime. Nobody at my company holds a personal Anthropic key, and if they did, they wouldn't be allowed to paste it into a website.

Q17Say you had one. What would security say about pasting it into a third-party web app?
PR

Our secrets policy is one line: credentials never go into third-party sites. A key is worse than the thread. If your page leaks it, anyone can spend on our bill, and anything sent with it goes out under our contract. Then they'd ask the question I'd ask: does the key go to your server? If it does, you're still a vendor, just one that's holding our key now. If it goes straight from my browser to Anthropic, that's better, but they'd still want to read the code, and the code is on your site, not in our repo.

Q18Would a key field break “no settings before the first reply” for you?
PR

If it's optional and folded away, like “Use your own Anthropic key,” I'd ignore it, and it wouldn't bother me. If it's required, it's the account gate with a different name, and I'd bounce on the first visit. But the bigger point is: an optional field I can't fill in doesn't build anything for me. It's for somebody else. A consultant with a personal Anthropic account, maybe.

Q19What about self-hosting: the code as a repo you run locally or inside your company, pointed at your company's key?
PR

That's the only version that could be legal for real material. If it runs on my laptop or in our infra and calls Anthropic with a key from Vault, it's an internal tool, not a vendor. Same path as Ravi's script: a platform ticket and a code review. That took him about two weeks. But I'm not doing that for a tool I haven't had one win with, and not four weeks before an RFC. Self-hosting is what I'd do after it's already saved me a week, not to find out whether it can.

Q20So if we had to pick one: key field, self-host, or say plainly “this is for sanitized text”?
PR

For this month, say it plainly. I'm already sanitizing. Tell me the tool is built for that, tell me what to strip, and fill in the rest of the notice. Self-host is a later conversation, and it only happens if the sanitized version wins first. The key field I'd skip. It costs you a setting and it doesn't unlock me.
